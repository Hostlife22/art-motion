# t1_3b1b · 3Blue1Brown Mathematical/Conceptual Visualization

> One-sentence grammar: **Only put "the thing you are talking about" on the black background. Use smooth deformation (Transform) to directly change the previous concept into the next one. Color = the name of the concept. There is almost no cut in the whole film.**
>
> Demonstration film: `scripts/engine/demos/t1_3b1b/` (rendering `render.py --film demos/t1_3b1b`) (8s, 3 shots) · Overview `assets/animation-grammars/t1_3b1b_overview.jpg` · Code `scripts/engine/demos/t1_3b1b/`

## ① Applicable scenarios

- **Use**: Talk about a mechanism "how to become something else step by step" - neural network forward propagation, attention, matrix transformation, gradient descent, probability distribution, algorithm process. The narration is most suitable for** action-type sentences** such as "Let's flatten this picture into a column" and "Each pixel is multiplied by a weight": each sentence corresponds to a Transform.
- **Usage**: The audience needs to "see mathematics" - the symbols in the formula correspond to the objects in the picture one-to-one (the W in the formula is blue, and the connecting lines are also blue).
- **Don’t use**: product launches, emotional/brand films (white lines on a black background are too cold and too slow); real trends in data (that’s t3’s job); narrations are opinions, stories, and golden sentences (there is no object that can be “transformed”, only decoration is left on the hard copy). Quick cuts within 8 seconds are also not suitable - the native rhythm of 3b1b is an animation of 1-3 seconds + a pause of 1 second (see ⑤), and 3 shots in 8 seconds is already the upper limit.

## ③ Motion paths

- **Easing**: default `smooth = 6t⁵−15t⁴+10t³` (3b1b version: zero velocity and acceleration at both ends; CE uses a sigmoid, `MO.smoothCE`). **Use it for almost every animation**. Reserve linear for Write, constant-speed rotation, and ShowPassingFlash progress. Use `there_and_back` for Indicate and `rush_into/rush_from` for rushing in/out.
- **Default duration**: Animation run_time=1s, wait=1s. In the 2023–24 source, the most common explicit run_time values are 2s (481 occurrences), 3s (333), 1s (187), and 4s (145).
- **LaggedStart**: default lag_ratio 0.05; 0.1 is most common in the source. Implementation: `MO.lagged(i, n, p, r)` (item i spans `[i·r, i·r+1]`, total length `1+(n−1)r`). This film uses 0.01 for 100 pixels transforming into neurons (1.1s total), and 0.006 for 160 connections.
- **Write**: linear; 1s for fewer than 15 subobjects, otherwise 2s; lag between subobjects = `min(4/(n+1), 0.2)`. Each glyph is outlined before it fills (`DG.math`/`DG.write`: advance the lineDash stroke, then fade the fill in and stroke out over the final 60%).
- **Transform**: resample both shapes to the same point count and **align their starting points** (upper left, clockwise), then interpolate points along straight paths (path_arc=0) and interpolate colors. Use `DG.rectPts/circlePts/morph`. Here: squares → circles (pixels → neurons), and weight cells → one neuron (weighted sum).
- **Entrances/exits**: FadeIn/FadeOut with shift (24–40px here, rising into view or fading upward); new-layer neurons enter from 24px to the right; replaced objects shrink to points and join the vertical ellipsis.
- **Signals**: ShowPassingFlash (time_width 2). Duplicate a connection in yellow, multiply its width by 1.5, and reveal `[max(0,u−2), min(1,u)]` as u advances from 0 to 3 (`DG.flash`); stagger input rows by lag 0.04.
- **Camera**: animate the MovingCameraScene frame. Shot 1 pushes slowly around the grid from 1→1.025; shot 2 pushes toward the output layer from 1→1.08. Orbit 3D scenes at 0.5–3°/s (3b1b default 1°/s).
- **Continuous subtle motion**: the originals usually remain **still during wait**. This is a major difference from keynote films. To satisfy continuous-motion requirements here, use only a very slow camera push (≤3% per shot), without breathing or floating.

## ④ Transitions (4 common types)

1. **Transform continuity (no cut)**: the last frame of one shot equals the first frame of the next, with old objects morphing into new ones. Here, shot 1→2 transforms 100 pixel squares into input neurons; the boundary frames are pixel-identical. This is 3b1b's dominant transition.
2. **Zoom through**: push exponentially around an object (`zoom = Z^e`, constant perceived speed); its circumference becomes an opening onto the next scene. `TRANSITIONS.zoomThrough` parameters: `live:true` (the old shot continues vector camera rendering beyond its duration), `r0` (object radius), `zoom ≥ 1100/r0` (otherwise the opening will not cover the screen and the final frame jumps), and `focus` (keep the next subject centered in the opening until e>0.55). Here, shot 2→3 outlines the hidden neuron with activation 0.95 in yellow, then zooms into it 48× over 0.75s.
3. **FadeOut(shift=UP) + FadeIn(shift=UP)**: on a topic change, old content fades upward and new content enters from below (`TRANSITIONS.fadeShift`).
4. **ReplacementTransform for titles/formulas**: move symbols in the old formula to matching symbols in the new formula (TransformMatchingTex, default 2s in the 3b1b version); unmatched symbols fade out/in.

## ⑤ Rhythm and text

- **Shot duration**: originals use continuous scenes with 1–3s animations followed by 1s waits; one concept often occupies 8–20s. This 8s film's three shots (2.6/2.8/2.6s) are **compressed**; extend them 2–3× for narration.
- **Narration alignment**: Grant lays out the animations before recording narration; after recording, editing takes roughly a day. Apply one sentence per animation: start on the sentence's action verb, settle by its end, then wait about 1s for viewers to read.
- **Text**: Write titles/questions before the action; introduce formulas alongside the visual using matching colors. Values such as 0.95 count with the fill, rather than appearing early. Keep English text to one title line plus one annotation line per screen.
- **FAQ principle**: every screen movement should have a clear explanatory purpose; remove motion that does not support the explanation.

## ⑧ The 3 most common things to do wrong

1. **Take "glow" as the logo of 3b1b and add bloom and deepen the blue gradient background.**  The original work is a pure black background + highly saturated thin lines + ×1.5 saturation, with almost no halo. Adding a halo turns it into a "technical sense PPT".
2. **Use linear or normal ease for faster and more animations.**  The default must be smooth (quintic polynomial), with an animation lasting 1–3s and then stopping for 1s; each action must correspond to a verb in the narration.
3. **Color coding changed midway/too many colors.**  One color = one concept, the symbols in the formula and the objects in the picture are of the same color; the audience will not be able to remember more than 3-4 semantic colors.
